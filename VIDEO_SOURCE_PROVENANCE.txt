CRITICAL — DO NOT USE THE SUPPLIED ANNOTATIONS
----------------------------------------------
This dataset ships with 3,520 polygon-annotated frames. Those annotations are the
authors' work, not mine.

Use the RAW VIDEO ONLY. Every track, box, keyframe and attribute in this portfolio
is my own, produced from scratch under my own guidelines (v6.0), with no reference
to the supplied labels.

State this explicitly in the portfolio. A dataset that arrives pre-annotated is a
place where an unclear claim looks like a dishonest one, and the whole point of this
work is that the judgment shown is mine.

Do not open the supplied annotation files at any point. Not having seen them is a
stronger statement than having ignored them.


CLIP SELECTION
--------------
Source clip:        GX020206_001150_001220  (Videos/Samples, 720p downscale)
Working file:       GX020206_TRACK_f350-649.mp4
Frames used:        350-649 of 899  (frame-exact trim, re-encoded CRF 18)
Frame rate:         29.97 fps
Resolution:         720 x 630
Duration used:      10.01 s / 300 frames
Full clip length:   30 s / 899 frames

Why this clip
-------------
All 31 clips are 30 s at 29.97 fps. Four carry the authors' polygon annotations
(GX010206, GX020019, GX020206, GX030013); the remaining 27 do not. Selection was
restricted to those four so that a blind comparison against independent annotation
remains possible.

Of the four:
  GX020019 - heavy haze, subjects close to the lens and soft. Most instances would
             fail the annotation threshold. Rejected.
  GX030013 - one very clear fish at ~frame 150, then long empty stretches. Too
             little to track. Rejected.
  GX010206 - workable, but fish are dark, low-contrast and clustered along the
             bottom of the frame, with heavy mutual occlusion throughout.
  GX020206 - selected.

Why frames 350-649
------------------
A well-lit tilapia traverses right to left across the frame through this window,
with a visible eye, readable fin margins and gold-green colouring, passing through
a loose group of fish behind it. That combination gives:

  - one primary track that can be held with confidence for its full span
  - genuine crossing events, which is where ID switches occur
  - real occlusion, which exercises the ID persistence rule
  - a background group dense enough that the annotation threshold does real work

Selected by sampling every 10th frame across the window and inspecting, before any
annotation began.

Trimmed to 10 s deliberately. 30 s at 29.97 fps is 899 frames, which is a long first
tracking job; a clip can be extended later, an afternoon cannot be recovered.

Viewing pass (after trimming, before annotating)
------------------------------------------------
All four annotated clips were watched end to end at speed to confirm the selection
made from sampled frames.

Confirmed: GX020206 is the strongest of the four, and the trimmed window contains
the traverse described above.

GX010206 reconsidered and rejected on a second look. It contains more fish passing
each other than GX020206 does, but they occur in the murkiest region of the frame,
where the instances are dark, low-contrast and mutually overlapping. Most would fail
the annotation threshold. **A crossing between two below-threshold instances
demonstrates nothing about identity persistence, because neither would carry a track
in the first place.** Rejected on that basis, not on appearance.

What the selected window does and does not contain
--------------------------------------------------
Contains: partial overlap between the primary tilapia and at least two other fish -
the primary's caudal region passing across a fish behind it, and a second fish's
fins crossing in front of the primary's head. This exercises `visibility` changing
along a track, boundary decisions under overlap, and ID persistence through partial
occlusion.

Does not appear to contain: a full occlusion in which an instance disappears
completely behind another and re-emerges. **The re-identification rule in guidelines
v6.0 is therefore expected to go unexercised in this batch**, in the same way the
occluded-keypoint flag went unexercised in the image run. This is recorded in advance
rather than discovered afterwards, and no attempt was made to select footage that
would flatter the rule set.

Resolution note
---------------
The 720p Samples version was used rather than the full-resolution Original. If the
comparison against the authors' annotations goes ahead, their polygons are in
Original coordinates and will need scaling by the resolution ratio before any IoU is
computed. Record the ratio at that point rather than assuming it.

SCOPE NOTE — 25 August 2026
---------------------------
300 frames is not 300 annotations. Under the v6.0 rule of a maximum 10-frame
keyframe interval, a 300-frame clip is ~30 keyframes per track. At three to five
annotatable tracks that is 90-150 box placements, not 900-1500.

Realistic budget for a careful job, including the end-to-end scrub of each track
required by the QC section: 3-5 hours. One long session or two evenings.

If that is still too long, the legitimate reduction is to TRIM THE CLIP, not to
thin the keyframes. Thinning keyframes below the 10-frame maximum breaks the rule
this project wrote and makes the track a claim nobody verified. Trimming to the
segment that actually contains the crossing and occlusion events preserves the
whole point of the video run, which is identity held through those events.

The reasoning is the same one already recorded for choosing one clip over several:
video demonstrates one thing, and repeating it is volume rather than evidence.
That logic applies inside a clip as well as across clips. "I selected the 150-frame
segment containing the two crossing events, because that is where identity is
actually at risk" is a stronger sentence than "I annotated 300 frames."

If the clip is trimmed, record here which frames were kept and why.
